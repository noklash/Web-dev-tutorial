# Session 2: Communicating With AI — The Art of Asking Powerful Questions

## Session Goal

By the end of this session, you should understand:

* Why the quality of your questions determines the quality of AI responses
* How to write effective AI prompts
* How to give AI enough information to produce useful answers
* How to turn AI into a tutor, coach, researcher, and creative partner
* How to improve your thinking through better communication

---

# 1. Introduction: AI Is Powerful, But It Cannot Read Your Mind

Many students try AI once and think:

> "AI is not that impressive."

Usually, the problem is not the AI.

The problem is the instruction.

Imagine walking into a restaurant and saying:

> "Give me food."

The waiter will probably ask:

* What type of food?
* How much?
* Any preferences?
* Any allergies?

The same thing happens with AI.

AI needs direction.

A vague question produces a vague answer.

A clear question produces a useful answer.

---

# 2. The Rule: Better Input Creates Better Output

A simple way to understand AI:

```
Your Instructions → AI Processing → AI Response
```

If your instructions are unclear:

```
Poor Prompt → Poor Response
```

If your instructions are detailed:

```
Good Prompt → Better Response
```

A person who knows how to communicate with AI has a major advantage.

---

# 3. What Is a Prompt?

A **prompt** is the instruction or question you give to an AI system.

Examples:

Simple prompt:

> Explain gravity.

Better prompt:

> Explain gravity to a 15-year-old student using everyday examples.

Advanced prompt:

> Act as a physics teacher. Explain gravity to a beginner student preparing for university. Use simple examples, diagrams described in words, and finish with five practice questions.

The third prompt gives AI a clear job.

---

# 4. The Five Parts of a Powerful Prompt

A strong prompt usually contains five elements.

## 1. Role

Tell AI who it should act as.

Examples:

* "Act as a mathematics tutor."
* "Act as a university professor."
* "Act as a career advisor."
* "Act as a programming mentor."

Why?

Because different experts explain things differently.

Compare:

### Without role:

> Explain programming.

### With role:

> Act as a senior software engineer teaching a beginner. Explain programming using real-world examples.

The second one creates better context.

---

# 2. Context

Context tells AI about your situation.

Example:

Weak:

> Help me learn chemistry.

Strong:

> I am a secondary school student preparing for university. I understand basic chemistry but struggle with chemical equations.

The AI can now adapt.

---

# 3. Task

Clearly explain what you want.

Examples:

Instead of:

> Tell me about biology.

Say:

> Explain the human respiratory system and how oxygen moves through the body.

---

# 4. Format

Tell AI how you want the answer.

Examples:

"Explain using:

* bullet points
* examples
* a table
* a quiz
* step-by-step instructions"

---

# 5. Goal

Explain what you want to achieve.

Example:

> My goal is to pass my university entrance examination. Focus on the concepts that are most important.

---

# The Complete Prompt Formula

A powerful prompt:

```
Role + Context + Task + Format + Goal
```

Example:

> Act as a university mathematics tutor. I am a beginner preparing for engineering school. Teach me calculus step by step. Use simple explanations, examples, and practice questions. My goal is to understand the fundamentals before university.

---

# 5. Turning AI Into Different Personal Assistants

AI can become many things depending on how you communicate.

---

# AI as a Teacher

Prompt:

> Act as my mathematics teacher. Explain quadratic equations from beginner level. After teaching, test me with five questions.

---

# AI as a Study Partner

Prompt:

> I will explain what I learned about photosynthesis. Identify mistakes and missing information in my explanation.

---

# AI as an Exam Coach

Prompt:

> Create a practice exam for biology at university entrance level. After I answer, grade my responses and explain my mistakes.

---

# AI as a Career Mentor

Prompt:

> Act as a career advisor. Ask me questions about my interests, strengths, and goals. Help me discover possible university fields.

---

# AI as a Writing Coach

Prompt:

> Review my essay. Do not rewrite it completely. Identify weaknesses and suggest improvements.

---

# 6. The Conversation Method: Do Not Ask Once and Leave

Many people use AI incorrectly:

They ask one question.

They receive an answer.

They stop.

Powerful users have conversations.

Example:

Student:

> Explain machine learning.

AI:

> Gives explanation.

Student:

> Explain that using an analogy.

AI:

> Gives analogy.

Student:

> Now test me.

AI:

> Creates quiz.

Student:

> Explain where I was wrong.

AI:

> Provides feedback.

This is how learning accelerates.

---

# 7. The "Five Whys" Learning Technique

When learning something difficult, keep asking:

"Why?"

Example:

Student:

> Why does the Earth orbit the Sun?

AI:

> Because of gravity.

Student:

> Why does gravity cause orbit instead of the Earth falling into the Sun?

AI:

> Explains balance between motion and gravity.

Student:

> Explain that using a simple analogy.

The deeper you question, the deeper you understand.

---

# 8. Common Prompt Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Being Too Short

Bad:

> Teach me coding.

Better:

> Teach me programming fundamentals as a beginner. Start with concepts, examples, and small exercises.

---

## Mistake 2: Not Giving Background

Bad:

> Explain economics.

Better:

> I am a high school student with no economics background. Explain the basic principles of economics.

---

## Mistake 3: Asking AI to Replace Thinking

Bad:

> Solve my assignment.

Better:

> Guide me through solving this problem and explain each step.

---

## Mistake 4: Trusting Everything

AI can make mistakes.

Always ask:

* "What are your sources?"
* "Are there alternative explanations?"
* "Check your answer."

# Key Lessons From Session 2

Remember:

1. AI is only as useful as the instructions you give it.
2. Great AI users are great communicators.
3. Give AI a role, context, task, format, and goal.
4. Treat AI like a conversation partner, not a search box.
5. The goal is not to get answers faster; the goal is to think better.
